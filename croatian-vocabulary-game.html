What you'll practise

The exercises cover over 150 everyday Croatian words that A2 learners use all the time. Every word comes with its English meaning. Nouns also show their gender and plural, and verbs their present and past forms.

  • Everyday basics: htjeti (to want), moći (can), trebati (to need), subota (Saturday), plav (blue)
  • Weather and feelings: hladno (cold), vruće (hot), oblačno (cloudy), pada kiša (it's raining), puše vjetar (the wind is blowing)
  • Place and intensity: ovdje (here), vani (outside), jako (very), malo (a bit), uskoro (soon)
  • Family and daily life: obitelj (family), doručak (breakfast), posao (job), ustajati (to get up), kuhati (to cook)
  • Travel and free time: vlak (train), karta (ticket), plaža (beach), putovati (to travel), rezervirati (to book)
  • Food and health: kruh (bread), povrće (vegetables), zdrav (healthy), bolestan (sick), spavati (to sleep)

Seven Croatian exercises

  • Speed quiz – pick the meaning of a word, from Croatian to English and back.
  • Memory pairs – turn over cards and match each Croatian word with its meaning.
  • Fill the gap – complete real sentences with the missing word, then read the translation.
  • Unscramble – put the letters back in order. LJ, NJ and DŽ count as one letter, as in the Croatian alphabet.
  • Odd one out – find the word that doesn't belong and learn the group the others share.
  • Which gender? – decide if a noun is masculine, feminine or neuter.
  • Past tense – choose the right past form for on (he) or ona (she).

Frequently asked questions

Is the Croatian vocabulary game free?

Yes. The game is completely free, with no sign-up and no account. Your miles and the words you got wrong are saved in your own browser.

What level is it for?

It is made for A2 (elementary) learners of Croatian. If you are at A1, it is a good challenge; at B1, it works as a quick review of everyday words.

How does the voyage from Rijeka to Dubrovnik work?

Each correct answer moves your boat 1 mile, and from 3 correct answers in a row each one is worth 2 miles. A round only counts if you make no mistakes; otherwise the boat sails back to the last port. About six perfect rounds take you all the way to Dubrovnik.

Why does the game show gender and plural?

Every Croatian noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and adjectives and past tense forms change with it: on je išao (he went), ona je išla (she went). That is why each noun shows its gender and plural, and each verb its present and past forms.
